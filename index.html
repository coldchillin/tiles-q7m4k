Singapore sets add four animal tiles to the usual 144. Tap any tile to see its name, how to say it, and what it does for your hand.

How a hand works

Build a hand of four sets and one pair (14 tiles), and make sure it is worth at least 1 tai. That's the whole goal. Everything else is how you get there.

The building blocks

Chow 吃 (say "chi"): three in a row of the same suit. Honours can't make chows.
Pong 碰: three identical tiles.
Kong 杠 (say "gang"): four identical. Counts as one set; you draw a replacement tile.
Eye 眼 / 將: the one pair every winning hand needs.
A complete hand: three chows, a pong of Red Dragon (1 tai) and a pair of South.

Seats, winds and the deal

  • Four players. The dealer sits East; play runs counter-clockwise East → South → West → North. In this app that means you, then the player on your right, across, and left.
  • Your seat number is 1 (East), 2 (South), 3 (West) or 4 (North). It decides which flower tiles and which wind are "yours".
  • Everyone takes 13 tiles; the dealer takes 14 and discards first.
  • Flowers and animals are never kept in the hand. Lay them face up and draw a replacement from the back of the wall straight away.

Your turn

  1. Draw one tile from the wall (replace any flower or animal).
  2. If it completes your hand, declare Hu 胡. Winning on your own draw is zi mo 自摸.
  3. Optionally declare a kong.
  4. Discard one tile face up. Your discards form your "river".

Claiming someone's discard

  • Chi only from the player on your left, the one who plays just before you.
  • Pong or kong from anyone. It jumps the turn order to you.
  • Hu beats every other claim. If two players can win, the one next in turn order takes it (some tables let both win).
  • After a chi or pong you expose the set, then discard. You do not draw.
  • Claimed sets are open. Only sets formed from your own draws stay concealed.

Kongs

  • Exposed kong 明杠: you hold three and claim the fourth from a discard.
  • Concealed kong 暗杠: you draw all four yourself. Shown with the ends face down.
  • Added kong 加杠: you draw the fourth tile of a pong you already exposed. If that tile would win for someone, they may rob the kong 搶杠 and you pay as the discarder.
  • Every kong earns an instant payment and a replacement tile from the back of the wall.

Ending a hand

  • The hand ends when someone wins, or when only the 15-tile dead wall is left. That is a draw, 流局.
  • A winning hand needs at least 1 tai. A hand with nothing scoring, sometimes called a chicken hand 雞胡, can't be declared at most tables.
  • Most tables cap a hand at 5 tai (the limit, 滿貫).
  • The dealer keeps the deal after a win or a draw. Otherwise the deal passes to the right. Once every player has dealt, the round wind moves on: East round, then South round, and so on.

The player whose discard you win on is the shooter. Many younger tables play shooter pays all instead: the shooter covers everyone's share and the other two pay nothing.

Instant payments

These are paid the moment they happen, whether or not you go on to win:

  • Bite 咬: holding Cat + Rat, or Rooster + Centipede. Everyone pays you.
  • Both of your seat flowers (the red and blue tiles with your number).
  • A complete flower set (all four of one colour) pays like a concealed kong.
  • Exposed kong pays like a bite; concealed kong pays double.

When the shooter pays for everyone 包

At many tables a careless discard makes you cover the whole win:

  • Feeding the third dragon pong to a player who already shows two dragon pongs.
  • Feeding a suit tile to a player showing three or four sets of that suit (an obvious flush).
  • Discarding a "fresh" tile (one not yet seen on the table) late in the hand, when it wins for someone.

Agree on these before you play

There is no single rulebook for Singapore mahjong. Check the minimum and limit tai, whether bites and kong payments are on, shooter-pays-all, and how Ping Hu is scored. The practice table here uses: 1 tai minimum, 5 tai limit, shooter pays double, self-draw all pay double, bites 2, exposed kong 2, concealed kong 4, complete flower set 4. Robbing the kong and the 包 rules are explained above but not enforced in practice games.

Playing better

The practice table's coach applies most of these in real time. Read them once, then watch for them while you play.

  1. Know where your tai will come fromYou can't win with 0 tai. Early on, check what you already have: animals, your seat flowers, a pair of dragons or of your wind. If you have none, steer toward Ping Hu, a flush or Pong Pong.
  2. Throw lonely honours first, but not all of themA single wind that is neither your seat nor the round wind can never score; discard it early while it is still safe. Hold a lone dragon or your own wind a little longer, since a second copy gives you a pair worth a pong.
  3. Middle tiles are worth more than edgesA 5 can join 3-4-5, 4-5-6 or 5-6-7. A 1 or 9 only fits one chow. When everything else is equal, discard terminals before middle tiles.
  4. Prefer two-sided waits4-5 waits on 3 or 6: up to 8 tiles.1-2 waits only on 3: 4 tiles at most.Ping Hu also needs this kind of open wait.
  5. Ping Hu is the big prize for a plain handNo flowers or animals and a hand of chows? Keep it all chows and don't pong anything: Ping Hu is 4 tai. Once you draw a bonus tile it drops to 1 tai, but by then the bonus tile itself pays.
  6. Claim selectivelyPong dragons and your scoring winds without thinking. Chi only when it clearly moves you closer to ready. Every open set shows the table your plan and leaves you fewer ways to finish.
  7. Lean into flushesWith eight or nine tiles of one suit, a Half Flush (2 tai) or Full Flush (4 tai) is often closer than it looks. Discard the other suits.
  8. Read the tableSomeone showing three sets of dots is hunting a flush: stop feeding dots. Someone showing two dragon pongs: never discard the third dragon, because you'd pay for everyone.
  9. Count what's goneIf three of the four tiles you're waiting on are already in the rivers, change your wait. The coach shows how many of your winning tiles are still live.
  10. Play safe lateWhen the wall is short, discard tiles that already appear in other players' rivers. A fresh tile late in the hand is the classic way to shoot a big hand.
